Scene 1 — The Oldest Temple
This is it, Yusuf. The biggest emergency yet.

Bob accidentally traveled to 10,000 BCE in southeastern Anatolia. He has stumbled into Göbekli Tepe, an ancient site that is widely considered the world's oldest temple.

Bob was wearing shiny sunglasses and holding an LED flashlight. Now, the local hunter-gatherers think Bob is a Sun God! They have trapped him in the center of the giant monolithic pillars. You have to save him, but opening a time portal here requires extreme precision.
Vocabulary Vault
The story describes Göbekli Tepe as "ancient" and "monolithic". What does "monolithic" mean?
💡 Hint: "Mono" means one, and "lith" means stone. What does that tell you about the pillars?
⏳ Vocabulary: Ancient — Belonging to the very distant past.
🗿 Vocabulary: Monolithic — Formed of a single, massive block of stone.
Scene 2 — The Epic Grammar Review
To activate the portal, you must speak the correct chronological incantation into your device. You have to use everything you've learned in the past four episodes: Past Continuous and Past Perfect!

Remember:
• Past Continuous (was + V-ing) = An interrupted action.
• Past Perfect (had + V3) = An action that happened BEFORE another action.
Grammar Challenge (Ultimate Review)
Which sentence correctly uses BOTH the Past Continuous and the Past Perfect?
💡 Hint: Look for a sentence that has "was/were [verb]-ing" AND "had [verb]ed".
Scene 3 — The T-Shaped Pillars
The portal machine powers on, but it needs a 3-digit access code based on the carvings around you.

Göbekli Tepe is famous for its T-shaped pillars carved with wild animals. You see three carvings: a Boar, a Fox, and a Vulture. You must figure out their numerical value.
Pillar Logic Rules
Rule 1 The Boar is worth exactly 1.
Rule 2 The Fox is worth twice as much as the Boar.
Rule 3 The Vulture is worth the Boar and Fox combined.
Logic Challenge: Code Breaking
You need to enter the code in this order: Vulture, Fox, Boar. What is the 3-digit code?
💡 Hint:
Boar = 1.
Fox = 2 * 1 = ?
Vulture = 1 + Fox = ?
Then arrange them: Vulture, Fox, Boar.
Scene 4 — History Repeats Itself
The portal whirs to life! It glows a bright, blinding blue.

The ancient hunter-gatherers fall to their knees, amazed by the light. You grab Bob by the collar and pull him into the portal. As you jump, Bob drops his sunglasses in the dirt.

You sigh. "Well, history repeats itself. Another tourist, another mess."
True or False — Idiom Check
Statement: If you say "history repeats itself," you mean that people often make the same mistakes or experience similar events over and over again.
💡 Hint: Bob always drops things in the past (iPhone, GPS, Chips, Sunglasses). Does he repeat his actions?
🔄 Idiom: History repeats itself — The idea that similar events or mistakes will continue to happen in the future.
