Scene 1 — The Spicy Mistake
Agent Yusuf, Bob is in hot water again.

He traveled to the 13th Century to walk the Silk Road in Anatolia. He met a caravan belonging to the famous explorer Marco Polo.

Bob got hungry and offered the traders a bag of modern, extremely spicy "Flamin' Hot Doritos" in exchange for some ancient silk. The traders ate the chips, and now they think they have been poisoned by dragon fire!
Idiom Check
The chief tells you that Bob is "in hot water." What does this idiom mean?
💡 Hint: Think about taking a bath in boiling water. Is that a comfortable situation?
🐪 Vocabulary: Caravan — A group of people, especially traders or pilgrims, traveling together across a desert.
🔥 Idiom: In hot water — To be in a difficult situation where you will be punished or criticized.
Scene 2 — Grammar Timeout: The Past Perfect
You arrive at the Caravanserai (an ancient roadside inn). The traders are coughing and crying. Marco Polo looks at you in panic.

When you act as a detective, you often have to talk about two things that happened in the past. To show which action happened first, we use the Past Perfect.
The Past Perfect Rule
Form Subject + had + Past Participle (V3)
Rule Use Past Perfect for the action that happened FIRST. Use Past Simple for the action that happened NEXT.
Example: "By the time Yusuf arrived (Past Simple), Bob had already eaten the chips (Past Perfect)."
Grammar Challenge
Marco Polo explains the disaster. Choose the correct sentence that shows Bob gave them the chips BEFORE the traders started coughing:
💡 Hint: Which action happened first? (Giving the chips -> Needs 'had given'). Which happened second? (Coughing -> Needs 'started').
Scene 3 — The Barter System
To calm the traders down, you must cut to the chase. You offer to buy back the spicy chips so they don't eat anymore.

But on the Silk Road, they don't use credit cards. They use a system of barter (trading goods for other goods). The merchant offers you a puzzle to complete the trade.
The Merchant's Trade Rules
Rule 1 1 Roll of Ancient Silk = 2 Bags of Saffron Spice.
Rule 2 1 Bag of Saffron Spice = 3 Silver Coins.
Logic Challenge: Trade Math
Bob traded 1 Roll of Ancient Silk for his chips. To buy it back, you need to pay in Silver Coins. Based on the rules, how many Silver Coins equal 1 Roll of Ancient Silk?
💡 Hint: 1 Silk = 2 Saffron. If each Saffron is 3 Coins, then 2 Saffron = (2 x 3) Coins!
✂️ Idiom: Cut to the chase — To get directly to the point without wasting time on unimportant details.
🤝 Vocabulary: Barter (noun/verb) — To exchange goods or services without using money.
Scene 4 — The Bitter Pill
You hand over 6 silver coins. The merchant smiles, but he is still holding his stomach. The spicy chips have infuriated his digestion!

You hand him a modern antacid tablet (stomach medicine). He looks at the white chalky disc suspiciously. You tell him it is a "magic healing stone," but he says it tastes terrible.

You whisper to Bob: "It's a bitter pill to swallow, but he has to eat it."
True or False — Idiom Check
Statement: If an experience is "a bitter pill to swallow," it means it is a sweet, enjoyable event that makes everyone happy.
😡 Vocabulary: Infuriated (adjective) — Extremely angry. (Can also mean highly irritated/upset, even for a stomach!)
💊 Idiom: A bitter pill to swallow — An unpleasant fact, situation, or consequence that must be accepted.
Scene 5 — Confiscation
The antacid tablet works! The merchant feels much better. He hands over the ancient silk, and more importantly, he hands over the bright red bag of Flamin' Hot Doritos.

Bob reaches out to grab his chips. You slap his hand away. "Not a chance, Bob. These are coming back to the 21st Century with me."

You open your time portal, waving goodbye to Marco Polo. The Silk Road is safe from artificial cheese flavoring!
